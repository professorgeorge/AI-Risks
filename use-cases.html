Scenario Walkthroughs
What it actually looks like in practice
Each scenario below describes a real planning situation and shows exactly which steps a person in that role would take inside Meridian.
01
Provost · Board Prep Meeting
"I need to show the board which programs are most at risk from AI — without a research deep-dive."
A provost has 20 minutes before a board meeting. The board has been asking questions about AI and curriculum. She needs a clear, defensible summary of where the institution's academic portfolio stands — without spending hours in spreadsheets.
1
Open the dashboard. The home screen shows all programs organized by field. No setup needed.
2
Click the ⚡ Urgent Pivot filter button. Every program that needs immediate curriculum action appears instantly.
3
Click the 🌅 Sunset filter. Review which programs have exposure so high that the underlying job market is already contracting.
4
Click 🖨️ Export PDF in the top navigation. A clean, dated summary report is ready for the board packet in one click.
✅
Result: The provost walks into the meeting with a credible, data-informed overview showing 8 programs in "Urgent Pivot," 3 in "Sunset," and 14 as strong "Invest" opportunities — drawn from federal research, not vendor claims.
02
Dean of Business · Annual Review
"I want to compare Accounting, Finance, and Marketing side by side and understand what's different between them."
A business school dean knows all three programs are being affected by AI, but doesn't understand why they might need different responses. She wants to see the differences concretely, in one view.
1
Click the 💼 Business field tile to filter the view to business programs only.
2
On each of the three program cards, click the small + Compare button that appears on hover. A blue tray appears at the bottom of the screen.
3
Click View Comparison → in the tray. A side-by-side panel opens showing exposure scores, runway years, faculty readiness, and task breakdowns for all three at once.
4
Notice that Marketing has a shorter runway (1.9 years) but higher faculty readiness (44%) than Accounting (1.8 years, 22% readiness). That changes the intervention priority.
✅
Result: The dean sees clearly that Accounting needs faculty development investment first — not because its AI risk is worse, but because the people who would teach a revised curriculum aren't ready yet. That's a different planning problem than Marketing.
03
Department Chair, Nursing · Curriculum Committee
"Someone told me AI is going to replace nurses. I need to know what's actually true and what we should actually change."
A nursing department chair has heard a lot of alarming headlines. Before going to the faculty with any proposed changes, she wants to understand the evidence and have a concrete list of what to actually do.
1
Type "Nursing" in the search bar at the top. The Nursing (BSN) card appears immediately.
2
Click the card. The detail drawer opens on the right side of the screen.
3
Read the Verdict box at the top — it says "Invest & Grow" with a plain-English explanation. AI exposure is 31%, with a 7.2-year runway. The recommendation is to expand, not contract.
4
Scroll to the Task Breakdown section. It shows that documentation and scheduling are somewhat affected (AI assists), while physical assessment, clinical judgment, and family communication are rated very low for AI impact.
5
Scroll to Recommended Actions. The most urgent item: add a module on interpreting AI-flagged patient risk scores. Not a program overhaul — a focused addition.
✅
Result: The chair goes to the faculty meeting with a specific, evidence-grounded proposal: one new module on AI documentation tools, expanded simulation hours, and a push to increase cohort size. The data supports the program, not the headlines.
04
VP of Enrollment Strategy · Portfolio Planning
"Which programs should we be actively recruiting into, and which ones should we quietly stop promoting?"
An enrollment officer is building the next three-year recruitment strategy. She needs to know which programs have strong long-term job market prospects — and which ones her team should stop marketing to prospective students in good conscience.
1
Click the 🌱 Invest & Grow filter. These are programs with low AI substitution risk and strong or growing employer demand. Prioritize recruitment and marketing spend here.
2
Note the trades programs (Welding, HVAC, Electrical Technology) — all show 11+ year runways and regional multipliers above 1.0. Often overlooked, but the data is clear.
3
Click the 🌅 Sunset filter. These programs have declining job markets and high AI substitution. Medical Billing & Coding (0.94 exposure, 0.8-year runway) is the starkest example.
4
Click any Sunset program and read the Recommended Actions. They tell you what to say to current students — transition pathways, bridge courses, adjacent programs.
✅
Result: The enrollment strategy now has a clear data rationale for where recruitment dollars go. The institution can also demonstrate to prospective students and their families that its program recommendations are grounded in labor market evidence, not just tradition.
05
Strategic Planning Office · Institutional Data Overlay
"Our enrollment numbers and faculty readiness look very different from the national averages. Can I see our specific picture?"
A planning analyst knows that the national defaults don't fully reflect his institution — they have unusually high enrollment in Nursing and unusually low faculty AI readiness in Business. He wants to see how those differences change the recommendations.
1
Click 📤 Import Data in the top navigation bar.
2
Click Download Blank CSV Template. The file opens in Excel or Google Sheets with all program names already listed and empty columns for enrollment, faculty readiness %, and regional multiplier.
3
Fill in the rows for the programs he has data on. Leave the rest blank. Save the file.
4
Drag and drop the saved file into the upload area, or click to browse and select it.
5
The dashboard instantly updates. Programs with institutional data now show a blue 🏛️ Your Institution badge. The scores are recalculated using the institution's actual figures instead of national defaults.
6
The data stays saved in the browser. He doesn't need to re-upload on the next visit.
✅
Result: The planning team now has a view that reflects their actual institution — not the national average. A program that looks stable nationally might look urgent when local enrollment and faculty readiness figures are plugged in, or vice versa.
06
Provost · "What If" Scenario Planning
"What if we weighted faculty readiness more heavily than task automation? Does that change which programs we prioritize?"
A provost leading a strategic planning retreat wants to show the cabinet that program risk depends on what your institution can actually do, not just on the national research. Her institution has a strong faculty development budget but limited capital for new technology infrastructure.
1
Click ⚙️ Methodology in the top navigation.
2
Read the Methodology Disclosure at the top of the panel — it explains exactly what the scores are based on and what they cannot tell you. Useful for any audience that will ask "where does this come from?"
3
Try the Faculty-Readiness Focus preset. This shifts weight heavily toward the faculty gap dimension. The scores recalculate instantly across all programs.
4
Compare the resulting recommendation list to the Balanced (Default) preset. Some programs move between categories. These are the programs worth the most discussion.
✅
Result: The cabinet meeting becomes a structured scenario conversation rather than an argument about which numbers to trust. Changing the weights visibly changes the output, which is itself a valuable insight: the right response to AI in higher education depends on your institution's specific constraints.

💡 Tips for Getting the Most Out of Meridian
📌
Start with the filters, not the search. If you don't have a specific program in mind, clicking "Urgent Pivot" or "Sunset" immediately surfaces the highest-priority conversations.
📌
The task breakdown is the most useful part of the detail drawer. It shows specifically which tasks AI is replacing versus augmenting, which tells you what kind of curriculum change is needed.
📌
Faculty readiness percentage is often the binding constraint. A program might urgently need to change, but if only 22% of faculty have AI fluency, the change timeline is controlled by faculty development, not just accreditation.
📌
The accreditation note in each drawer tells you your realistic runway. A 5-year AACSB cycle means curriculum changes formalized now won't be locked in until the next review. Begin pilot electives before then.
📌
Treat the scores as starting points, not verdicts. The Methodology panel's disclosure section explains exactly what is sourced versus estimated. Use these numbers to structure the conversation, then validate with your own institutional research.
📌
Your uploaded CSV data stays saved. Once you import institutional figures, they persist in your browser. You don't need to re-upload the next time you open the tool on the same device.
Common Questions
Frequently asked
Do I need to create an account or log in?
No. Meridian requires no account, no login, and no installation. Open the file in any browser and it works immediately.
Is my institution's data sent anywhere when I import a CSV?
No. All data processing happens entirely in your browser. Nothing is transmitted to any server. Your institutional figures are saved only in your browser's local storage and never leave your device.
How precise are the exposure scores? Can I cite them in a formal report?
The scores are directional indicators, not certified measurements. The underlying research framework is peer-reviewed and the source data is federal and publicly available, but the program-level figures involve methodological judgment. They are appropriate for strategic planning conversations and internal documents. For formal published work or accreditation submissions, treat them as a starting point and supplement with your own institutional research.
My program isn't in the database. What do I do?
You can use the "Import Data" feature to add a program manually, or find the closest equivalent in the database and note the parallels. The team behind Meridian is actively expanding the program database.
Can I share the PDF report externally?
Yes. The exported PDF is a clean document suitable for board packets, committee meetings, or sharing with department chairs. It includes the date and a note on the data sources. We recommend including a brief note that scores are directional indicators based on national research.
How often is the program data updated?
The underlying research sources (BLS, O*NET, IPEDS) are updated on annual or biennial cycles. Meridian aims to reflect the most current published data. Check the Methodology panel for the version and source dates.
