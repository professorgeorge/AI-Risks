      How the scores are built — and what they cannot tell you

      The framework is grounded in published research and federal data. The five sources below are real, public, and widely cited.

      Directly sourced: occupation-level AI exposure from Eloundou et al. and from Felten, Raj & Seamans; O*NET task statements; BLS employment projections; the NCES CIP–SOC crosswalk. Where a program's employment figure carries a BLS verified tag, that number was read from the current Occupational Outlook Handbook entry for the named SOC code.

      Estimated, not sourced: figures tagged Estimate. Program-level exposure percentages, curriculum half-lives and faculty-readiness figures are informed estimates derived from those sources. Translating a national occupational score into a program-level score requires judgement. Treat them as directional.

      Designed for: structuring a strategic conversation and deciding where to look harder. Not a substitute for formal institutional review with audited data.

      Cannot do: predict AI development, or account for your institution's particular strengths, employer relationships, accreditation position, or budget politics.

      Currency: a fixed snapshot. Employment projections, exposure research and O*NET task data all move. Programs near a category boundary can change category within a year. Review against updated sources at least every 18 months.
